Cross-cutting method

Choices that apply everywhere: what "CO₂e" means here, how a spending window becomes an annual rate, and how review effort is triaged.

Goods & Services

Spend-based accounting: each transaction is classified to a NAICS industry and its 2022-dollar spend is multiplied by that industry's economy-wide carbon intensity. Powerful because a bank export covers everything you buy; blunt because a dollar is a dollar — the model can't see brands or your specific cart. The full factor table is browsable at the industry table.

Travel

Physical models: miles and MPG rather than dollars. Vehicle and transit factors follow the CoolClimate household calculator (measured directly from their API so the app reproduces their model exactly, bugs included); air follows EPA per-haul factors.

Home — electricity

The most engineered corner of the app: your ZIP's grid, hour by hour, weighted by when households actually use power. The design decision underneath everything else is the first entry — everyone gets the physical grid average. The full technical writeup with validation tables lives in the gridcarbon README.

Home — gas, oil, structure

The non-electric home: combustion fuels at CoolClimate factors, the building itself amortized, and one deliberate omission.

Food

Diet-based, not spend-based: what you eat drives the number, and your grocery and restaurant spending serves only as a cross-check. Food transactions are excluded from Goods & Services so nothing is counted twice.

Offsets & credits

Subtractions, all softer than the emissions they offset: purchased removal at face value, and EPA avoidance equivalencies for recycling and compost.

Damages, deaths & benchmark

The "so what" numbers on the impact cards: what your emissions cost other people, what they do to mortality, and what the average US household looks like next to yours.

Data handling

Not a carbon assumption, but a promise worth documenting in the same register.
